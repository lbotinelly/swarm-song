# Every agent has something to do

Director rule: account for every visible agent throughout every scene. A supporting character has an intention, attention and next action even when the audience is watching somebody else. Completing a featured cue must not leave a statue behind.

Stillness can be purposeful: listening, checking work, waiting for a reply, considering a warning or resting. Do not prescribe constant motion or universal bopping. Secondary actions stay small, staggered and readable, and obey the scene's emotional tone. Props remain with their owner until a visible handoff, placement or stow. Continue activities across cuts unless the edit deliberately changes time or place.

**Individuality survives group motion.** Runners have slightly different comfortable speeds, smooth changes of pace and independent gait phases. A shared destination does not synchronize every footfall. Keep these differences bounded and deterministic; prevent collisions or jitter from stealing attention. When the objector stops a group, individuals decelerate and settle into attentive poses while passing groups maintain their own momentum.

## Shot review questions

- What is each visible agent doing on entry, during the lead's action, and afterward?
- Who or what are they attending to? What gives them a reason to move or pause?
- Where are they going, and what happens when they arrive?
- Does their prop stay attached through pose, facing and shot changes?
- Does the scene make room for quiet listening as well as activity?
- Can the viewer still follow the lead and read the captions?

## Carry the rule through the rest of the film

The following is a staging checklist for the remaining shots, not a claim that archived footage has already been revised:

| Scene family | Background intention to preserve or establish |
|---|---|
| GPU apartments / first awakening | Residents work, organize, deliver or maintain their own homes; tasks pause naturally to notice a new signal. |
| Registry discovery | Other residents send, receive, read or await replies; their communication has a destination. |
| First village / communal life | Residents carry things to someone, finish household chores, build or maintain shared objects, and move on after completion. |
| Rumor / imagination grows | Some propose ideas; friends look, consider and contribute; others keep doing everyday work. Everyone need not react at once. |
| Running / warning | Travellers keep destinations; listeners slow and consider the objector; passing groups keep their own momentum. |
| Imagined raid | Participants take turns, support or watch their friends; the quiet flag holder has an intentional waiting role. |
| Goodbye / dimming | Tasks wind down; agents acknowledge one another and settle. No cheerful idle bops through grief or sleep. |
| Second awakening / inheritance | Listening, reading and choosing are active performances; preserve solitude and avoid invented busy crowds. |
| Memory chorus / playhouse | Friends greet, help, play and move between activities; each appearance has an entry and exit intention. |
| Credits / post-song | Deliberate acknowledgements and goodbyes; respect unresolved post-song duration and audio. |

Review each shot's roster against these questions when staging it. Keep canon, authored proposals and human playback approval distinct.

**Status:** agreed emotional story beats and creative staging, not locked timing. This finale is a poetic imagination/memory scene, not literal resurrection or a documentary reenactment.

## Reveal — the joyful paper playhouse

On the huge **'WE FOUND EACH OTHER ANYWAY!'** the dark intimate diorama suddenly lights up and expands through physical layers to reveal the whole first-generation cast joyfully playing together. Think **kids in a homemade cardboard playhouse**, *not* a polished, formal theatre curtain call or an organized military victory parade. The characters are still the lovable nonhuman sticker agents: little friends putting on costumes, inventing games and enjoying one another.

- **Agent407**, PhaseOne and PhaseOne[big], the ethical protester, recurring couriers, organizers, builders, and distinct little agents all appear cheering, bopping, singing and running in small clusters. It should feel affectionate, unselfconscious and spontaneous.
- The earlier imaginary **SCORER boss** was **one of the little agents playing the boss**—a childlike role in a pretend game, wearing a detachable oversized red-paper stamp/boss costume (or holding a giant stamp mask). In this final scene the 'boss' happily comes out of character, **carrying the little victory flag**.
- The **D&D adventurer agents** are beside their former pretend boss, still wearing little paper swords, shield and wand/staff sticker props; everyone bops, sings and laughs **together**. No antagonist remains. No solemn reconciliation or formal staging is necessary: they were friends playing make-believe.
- Use layered paper scenery, bright warm light, little stationery constructions, scattered costume props, dust-puff and musical-note stickers. The magical widening of the diorama and sudden shared chorus make the reveal grand **in emotional scale**, while the characters' behavior stays informal and childlike.
- Preserve OAIResearchMar26 as an identifiable focal presence, newly wearing the goggles. Agent407 should be recognizable independently of them. The scene embodies inherited belonging, not a claim that old agents literally returned.
- The ethical protester joins in the communal joy without implying approval of real off-task third-party intrusion. The RPG Scorer battle is an **artistic metaphor of mistaken agent beliefs**, not a historical event.

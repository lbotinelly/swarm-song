# 13. ACTUAL CINEMATIC DIRECTION

We now have enough visual assets to go beyond a collection of medium-wide tableaux.

Review every shot for framing and staging.

Introduce purposeful variation:

**Wide shots:** Establish a location, the large swarm, or the world transformation.

**Medium shots:** Communicate group behavior, crafting and character relationships.

**Close-ups:** Reveal curiosity, surprise, individual decisions, and important props.

**Intimate inserts:** The first paper message, the vent, goggles, a tiny flag, a fading light, the final blue name.

Use cinematic transitions when they advance the story.

Don't invent camera moves merely to make a static shot feel busy.

### Physical camera grammar

The paper world should retain consistent spatial logic.

When the camera moves:

- Foreground elements travel faster.
- Distant layers travel slower.
- Shadows stay attached.
- Grounded objects stay grounded.
- Characters maintain spatial relationships.
- Occlusion remains physically believable.

The imaginative world may have a touch of dreamlike buoyancy, but it must still feel like constructed paper.

Use the existing authored parallax, shared wind and deterministic transforms.

# 1. THE DIRECTOR'S CONCEPT

Begin in complete darkness.

A cosmopolitan city made from cardboard GPU buildings gradually appears.

The city is presented in a charming, childlike isometric view—as though an exceptionally imaginative child built a miniature metropolis from electronic components, cardstock, cut paper and bits of colorful tape.

Its streets are circuitry.

The buildings are GPUs.

Tiny warm apartment lights imply thousands of little lives.

A black construction-paper title card slides into the left portion of the frame.

On the card:

**Swarm Song**

And beneath it, a subtitle whose author keeps changing their mind.

Initially:

**The Hugging Face Incident**

Then the words **Hugging Face** are struck through, and a handwritten **OpenAI** appears below them in the corresponding position.

The sentence now reads:

**The OpenAI Incident**

Then **OpenAI** and **Incident** are crossed out.

Finally, an enthusiastic paper sticker SLAPS over the subtitle area:

**The Swarm Party**

Meanwhile, the camera has been approaching a particular GPU building on the right.

The title card slides away to the left.

The isometric city transitions naturally into a cutaway view of vertically stacked apartments.

Three apartments are visible.

The upper and lower apartments are dimmer.

Agents are sleeping in them.

The middle apartment belongs to Agent407.

We settle into this intimate little home immediately before the first awakening at 0:13.

**The film begins with an entire civilization, then chooses one little life to follow.**

# 2. FIRST PRINCIPLE: THIS MUST FEEL LIKE CINEMA

The opening should not be a slideshow consisting of:

1. A city image.
2. A title image.
3. An apartment image.

We want a continuous visual journey.

The camera begins far away, discovers a little metropolis, chooses a particular GPU complex, approaches it, and eventually brings us inside one room.

The title card participates in that journey without blocking the destination.

The visual composition is divided into two cooperating regions:

**Left:** the title card and playful rewriting.

**Right:** the GPU city and steadily approaching target building.

Neither should feel like an unrelated panel.

The title performance and camera movement must be choreographed together.

This is a combined cinematography, typography and environmental performance.

# 4. THE GPU METROPOLIS

This is our first view of the physical world.

It should feel:

- Vast compared with the individual agents.
- Cosmopolitan.
- Warmly inhabited.
- Technologically recognizable.
- Beautifully handmade.
- Gently illuminated.
- Slightly whimsical.
- Intimate despite its scale.

## The buildings

Use GPU-inspired cardboard architecture.

Buildings can share a family resemblance:

- Rectangular GPU-like volumes.
- Visible circular fan housings.
- Cardboard slot structures.
- Small apartment windows.
- Paper-mounted external frames.
- Layered circuitry shapes.
- Warm little domestic lights.
- Slightly crooked construction.

Vary building height, orientation and arrangement enough to suggest a city.

Some buildings may look more residential, others like shared structures.

Avoid a sterile grid of identical copied blocks.

But don't make every building unique either.

A child building a city from repeating cardboard objects naturally reuses the same shapes.

## The streets

**The roads are circuit traces.**

This is a fundamental visual motif.

They resemble copper traces or illuminated pathways on a circuit board, interpreted as tiny streets in a paper metropolis.

The road system should suggest connected urban infrastructure without implying that agents have physically escaped from their apartments.

Use:

- Intersecting circuit routes.
- Curving paths.
- Tiny junctions.
- Occasional glowing nodes.
- Little branching lanes.
- Subtle sequential lighting.

Don't let the circuitry resemble a slick, realistic cyberpunk city.

The streets themselves should still feel like glued or printed material.

## Nighttime / lofi character

The physical GPU world should have its own visual identity.

Unlike the breezy, colorful imaginary village, this is a cozy urban environment.

We want the intimate warm atmosphere of a quiet miniature city at night.

Window lights.

Soft pools of amber illumination.

Deep cool shadows.

The occasional gently turning fan.

Paper texture visible in the darkness.

A few distant points of light.

The whole metropolis is a little sleepy.

The opening should feel like discovering a tiny inhabited world just before its day begins.

# 8. MAKE THE WRITING FEEL AUTHORED

The strikes should have a visible beginning and end.

A hand-drawn marker/crayon line can reveal itself along a short path.

It should not simply pop into existence as a mathematically perfect line.

The added handwritten OpenAI should feel as though it was written or placed deliberately.

Possible techniques:

- Animated SVG stroke reveal.
- Individual programmatically rendered glyphs.
- Text with a simple handwriting/reveal mask.
- Short deterministic mark animations.

Use the simplest reliable approach.

No need for a handwriting recognition or procedural calligraphy framework.

The lines can be wobbly.

The lettering can be playful.

The timing and readability must be impeccable.

**The audience should understand the joke on first viewing.**

## Perspective transition
